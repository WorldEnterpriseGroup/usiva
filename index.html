Companion role

US Fellows builds the research pathway. USIVA helps protect the visa posture.

The two sites link together because scholars need both: a fundable academic strategy and a clean, careful compliance plan.

What USIVA adds

Clear planning before the scholar makes expensive moves.

International candidates often focus on admission first and immigration later. USIVA moves the compliance questions earlier so the scholar understands evidence, timing, risk, and authorization boundaries before the application cycle becomes urgent.

  • 01

    Document readiness

    Passport, identity records, transcripts, English proficiency evidence, credential evaluations, funding proof, prior visa history, and university-specific requirements.

  • 02

    Student visa posture

    F-1 planning, I-20 readiness, SEVIS fee timing, DS-160 preparation, interview narrative, non-immigrant intent, and financial documentation.

  • 03

    Authorized research work

    Assistantship, on-campus work, CPT, OPT, STEM OPT, sponsor rules, school DSO coordination, and the difference between training, study, and employment.

Compliance first

Good research planning never outruns visa rules.

USIVA keeps the scholar focused on authorized activity, university process, documentation quality, and written approvals. No website page can replace individualized legal advice, and no program can guarantee an immigration outcome.

01

Before admission

No U.S. student work authorization exists simply because a scholar is preparing a PhD packet. Remote activity, volunteering, compensation, and training should be reviewed carefully.

02

During F-1 study

Full course of study, assistantship duties, on-campus employment, CPT, and other activity must be coordinated through the university and its designated school official.

03

After the PhD

OPT and STEM OPT are possible only when eligibility, timing, degree relationship, employer requirements, reporting, and government rules are satisfied.

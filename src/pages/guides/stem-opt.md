# STEM OPT readiness is a three-party compliance plan

STEM OPT adds another layer to the F-1 practical-training conversation. The student, employer, and school must be able to explain the degree connection, the training opportunity, the employer facts, and the reporting record. A STEM-sounding job title or a general technology degree is not the whole analysis.

> **Information boundary.** This page is informational planning content. It is not legal advice and does not guarantee admission, employment, funding, visa issuance, work authorization, or government approval. It does not replace individualized advice from qualified immigration counsel, the employer’s authorized representative, or the school’s DSO. Rules, forms, fees, and local instructions change; verify the current official guidance before acting.

## Start with the four-way alignment

The [DHS STEM OPT Hub](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub) is the primary public starting point for current program resources. It should be read with the [DHS Form I-983 Overview](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/form-i-983-overview), the [DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List](https://www.ice.gov/sites/default/files/documents/stem-list.pdf), the [ICE Practical Training resource](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/practical-training), and the current [USCIS Form I-765 Instructions](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-765instr.pdf).

Use those official sources to test four separate connections:

1. **Degree:** the degree record and CIP information must be checked against the current designated STEM list and the school’s record.
2. **Employer:** the employer’s legal identity, E-Verify facts, worksite, supervision, compensation, and ability to provide the proposed training must be documented.
3. **Training plan:** the work-based learning opportunity must connect to the qualifying degree and describe goals, supervision, resources, and evaluation in the current Form I-983 framework.
4. **Reporting:** the student, employer, and DSO need a reliable way to track required evaluations, material changes, contact details, and status questions.

The DSO and official agencies determine how the facts apply. This page only helps the team bring the same facts to the conversation.

## Assign ownership before collecting signatures

| Record or question | Student keeps | Employer confirms | DSO or official source verifies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qualifying degree and CIP code | Diploma, transcript, program record, and school-issued CIP information | — | DSO and current DHS STEM list |
| Employer identity | A copy of the offer or employment record | Legal name, E-Verify information, worksite, supervisor, and compensation | DSO and current USCIS/DHS instructions |
| Degree relationship | A plain-language explanation tied to actual duties | Job description, tools, outputs, and supervision | DSO; counsel if the relationship is disputed |
| Training plan | Copy of the current signed plan and evaluation dates | Goals, learning activities, supervision, resources, and evaluations | DSO and current Form I-983 resources |
| Changes and reporting | Personal calendar and dated change log | Prompt notice of material changes or end of employment | DSO and the current STEM OPT Hub |

An empty field is a question to resolve, not permission to guess. Keep the document source and date next to every answer.

## What makes a training plan credible?

The [Form I-983 Overview](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/form-i-983-overview) explains that the student and employer complete the plan together. It describes a plan that connects practical training to the qualifying STEM degree, states specific goals, explains how work-based learning will achieve those goals, and records supervision and evaluation.

For editorial planning, that means the work should be described as a learning relationship with observable duties—not as a generic job description copied from a careers page. Record:

- the technical or research knowledge the student will use;
- the skills or techniques the student is expected to develop;
- who supervises the work and how feedback happens;
- where the work is performed and how the employer supports it;
- what the evaluation points are; and
- how an actual change would be reported and reviewed.

Do not create facts to make a role fit. If the plan cannot describe the connection truthfully, stop and ask for professional review.

## Change triggers deserve their own log

Ask the DSO whether a revised plan, updated record, or other action is required when there is a new employer, a change in ownership or employer identity, a new worksite, a material change in duties or compensation, a substantial change in hours, a new supervisor, a break in employment, or a change in the student’s degree or status record. The current STEM OPT Hub and school instructions control the response.

Remote or partner-site work also needs a precise description. “Remote” is not a substitute for recording where day-to-day duties occur, who directs them, and how supervision and training will operate.

## A STEM OPT evidence packet is not a universal filing packet

Use the [Evidence & checklists guide](/guides/evidence-checklists.html) to index records, but verify live requirements before relying on them. The planning file may include:

- the current degree record and CIP information;
- the current EAD and F-1/OPT status records;
- the employer’s legal identity and E-Verify information;
- the detailed role description and degree-relationship explanation;
- the current Form I-983 and supporting training-plan materials;
- supervisor, worksite, evaluation, and reporting contacts; and
- a dated log of changes, questions, DSO responses, and official-source checks.

The [USCIS OPT page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students) and [Form I-765 Instructions](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/i-765instr.pdf) control current filing details. This guide intentionally does not reproduce a filing sequence, form completion instructions, or an application workflow.

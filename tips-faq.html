Tips & FAQ

Questions worth asking before you send the form.

A short answer can orient you. It cannot turn a general page into a personal eligibility decision. Use this FAQ to decide what to check, who to ask, and when to stop guessing.

Common questions

Start with the answer, then check the source.

These responses describe a planning posture. They are not legal advice, a government decision, or a promise that any program or status is available.

What is a readiness file?

A working set of identity, academic, funding, travel, prior-visa, and program records that helps you see what is known, missing, dated, or still needs verification. The right contents depend on the school, sponsor, program, and decision.

Does a complete file mean I am eligible?

No. Organizing documents can make a conversation clearer, but a school, sponsor, consular officer, government agency, or licensed immigration attorney may control a different part of the decision. Completeness is not approval.

Who should answer my university or status question?

Start with the written instructions for your program and the designated school official or sponsor named for your situation. If the question turns on personal history, status, or legal interpretation, consider licensed immigration counsel.

Are study, research, training, and employment the same activity?

No. They may involve different permissions, documentation, and timing. Name the actual activity, who will direct it, whether compensation is involved, and whether written authorization is required before it begins.

Can I begin work while I wait for an answer?

Do not assume that submitting a form, receiving an invitation, or being interested in a role authorizes activity. Ask the responsible school, sponsor, government source, or attorney before beginning anything that may be training, research, or employment.

Does USIVA issue a visa or guarantee an outcome?

No. USIVA public content is informational planning content. It does not guarantee admission, employment, funding, visa issuance, or government approval.

Why do sources and dates matter?

Rules, fees, school instructions, sponsor requirements, and program details can change. A source that applies to one status, institution, location, or date may not answer another question. Save the source and the date you checked it.

When is general content not enough?

When the answer depends on a prior refusal, status concern, criminal or immigration history, a complicated work arrangement, a change of status, or another personal fact that a public page cannot evaluate. Prepare the facts and use the authority responsible for the matter, including licensed counsel when appropriate.

Are prices published on USIVA?

There is no public USIVA price list on this site, and this page does not request payment. Confirm the provider, scope, terms, and limits directly before sending money or sensitive information.

Verify before you act

A four-question pause.

Use it whenever a page, person, or program sounds more certain than the source supports.

  1. 01What exactly is the activity?

    Study, training, research, travel, assistantship, internship, or employment?

  2. 02Who controls the answer?

    School, sponsor, agency, consulate, employer, or licensed attorney?

  3. 03What source is current?

    Record the official page or written instruction and the date checked.

  4. 04What should wait?

    Do not begin a questionable activity or send sensitive records while the boundary is unclear.
